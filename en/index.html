How to start touch typing

Typing without looking is a motor skill: you do not understand it, you train it. These eight steps are everything you need to know before the first lesson.

  1. Tell it which keyboard you have

    The on-screen keyboard draws the layout you pick, not the one your computer is set to. If they disagree, it will teach you keys that are somewhere else. You set it in the ⚙ button at the top right, and it is remembered in this browser.

    • Layout: which character each key types. It has to be the one your operating system is set to, which is not always the one printed on the keys. To settle it, press the key to the right of L: if you get ñ, it is the Spanish one.
    • Keyboard type: only changes the drawing — full size, laptop, Mac, split, ortholinear… Pick the one that looks like yours. On columnar split and Apple boards it also changes which finger owns each key.
    • Language: the one the page is written in, independent from the keyboard. You can practise the Spanish keyboard with the site in English. Each language has an address of its own, so a link you share opens in the language you were using.

    The lessons come from both, in this order: first from what your keyboard can type, then from the language of the page. A Spanish keyboard fits both courses, so the language decides; an English one always gets the English course, because the Spanish one is full of ñ and accents that keyboard does not have.

  2. Set up your desk

    Good posture prevents injuries and, along the way, removes mistakes.

    • Back against the chair and feet flat on the floor.
    • Elbows close to your body, at roughly 90°.
    • Wrists in the air and straight: the fingers move, not the arms.
    • Top of the screen at eye level, about an arm away.
    • Keyboard flat or barely tilted, centred with the space bar in front of you.

  3. Put your hands on the home row

    The home row is the resting position: the eight non-thumb fingers always sit there and come back to it after every keystroke.

    Left hand
    Right hand

    Find the two bumps on the F and J keys: they are the tactile signal that lets you place your hands without looking. Rest your index fingers there, let the rest fall into place and keep the thumbs over the space bar.

  4. Every finger owns its territory

    Each key is always pressed with the same finger, and that finger returns to its home key. Hover (or tap) a finger to see which keys it takes care of.

    Home row keys carry an underline. The index fingers are the only ones that stretch to two columns: G and H are theirs.

  5. Thumbs only press the space bar

    Nothing else. The usual advice is to always use the same thumb (the one on your dominant hand) and leave the other resting. Space is the most frequent key in any text: hitting it with a random finger costs you the hand position on every word.

  6. Capitals: Shift with the opposite hand

    For a letter on the left hand, hold ⇧ with the right little finger, and the other way round. Never with the same hand that presses the letter: it twists your wrist and costs you the home row. Caps Lock is not used to write text.

    On the on-screen keyboard the letter and the Shift you need light up together.

  7. How to practise

    • Do not look at your hands. Cover them with a cloth the first few days if you need to.
    • Accuracy before speed. Speed arrives on its own; learnt mistakes do not leave on their own.
    • Steady rhythm. Slow and regular beats bursts: your ear tells you when the rhythm breaks.
    • 10-15 minutes a day. Every day is worth far more than two hours on Sunday.
    • Always return to the home row after every keystroke, even if it takes longer.
    • Repeat the lesson until three stars before moving on.

  8. Common mistakes

    • Watching the on-screen keyboard instead of the text: use it only when you get lost.
    • Rushing before mastering the lesson: below 95% accuracy, slow down.
    • Hitting the keys hard; brushing them is enough.
    • Resting your wrists while typing.
    • Always using the same Shift, or dodging the little finger with the one next to it.
    • Looking down "only for the numbers": they are part of the course too.

What speed to expect

WPM is words per minute, counting five keystrokes per word. Writing by hand is about 20 WPM; hunting and pecking with two fingers, between 25 and 30.

wpm Level
15-25You already type without looking. The goal of the first blocks.
30-40Fluency: you type almost as fast as you think the sentence.
50-60Solid professional level.
70+Fast: from here on accuracy rules, not the fingers.

Start with the tutorial

Eight steps, three minutes to read, covering what no single lesson teaches on its own:

  • Posture: chair, elbows, wrists and screen height.
  • The home row: where the hands rest and how to find it again without looking, by feeling for the bumps on F and J.
  • Which keys belong to each finger, on your own keyboard in colour, with the hand guide.
  • Capitals with the opposite hand and one thumb dedicated to the space bar.
  • How to practise and the mistakes beginners repeat the most.
